Method

Intent-Centered Design

I design AI products around what the user is trying to do — not around the model, and not around a chat box.

David Cervantes·AI product leader, designer, and strategist

Chat is an interface, not a strategy

Most AI products start with a text box and hope the user knows what to ask. That works in a demo. It fails the moment a real person sits down with a real goal and a half-formed question. The model is capable; the product is a blank page. The user carries all the load.

Intent-Centered Design starts from the other end. I design around the intent — the thing the person is actually trying to accomplish — and I make the product meet them there.

What it means

Intent-Centered Design is a way of designing AI products around the user's intent rather than the model's capabilities or a generic conversation. You map the moments where a person forms a goal, and you surface the right capability at that moment — before they have to phrase it, dig for it, or even know it exists.

Three things it asks of every screen:

  • Name the intent. What is the person here to do, right now? If I can't say it in one sentence, the screen isn't ready.
  • Surface at the moment. The capability appears when the intent forms — not three menus away, and not only if you type the magic words.
  • Explain and defer. The system says what it's doing, what it's unsure of, and when it hands back to a human. Trust is part of the design, not a disclaimer at the bottom.

Why it works

A trader doesn't want a chatbot. They want to know why a setup is forming and whether the odds are real — at the moment it forms. A foreign buyer doesn't want a search form in a second language. They want the one property that fits, explained plainly. Design around the intent and the AI stops being a party trick; it becomes something a real team uses every day.

I've built this across fintech, real estate, and construction. The method holds each time: study how people actually judge and decide, then shape the product around that gap — through research, design, and working code.

The short version

Design for the intent, not the interface. The model is the engine. Intent is the map.
