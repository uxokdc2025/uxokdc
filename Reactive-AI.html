Reactive AI

AI that responds to what's happening — surfacing the right thing at the right moment, instead of waiting to be asked.

David Cervantes·AI product leader, designer & strategist

Most AI waits

Most AI sits behind a prompt. It does nothing until you type, and it only reacts to the words. That's fine for a search box. It's thin for a product someone works inside all day, where the important thing is rarely the thing they thought to ask.

Reactive AI starts from the situation, not the sentence. It watches the state of the work and acts the moment something matters.

What it means

Reactive AI is AI that responds to the state of the work — surfacing an insight, a warning, or an option the instant it becomes relevant, without the user having to ask. The prompt still exists. It's just no longer the only way in.

Three things it asks of the system:

  • Watch the state. Know what's changing — the position, the schedule, the document, the signal — not just the last message.
  • Act at the moment. Raise the right thing when it's true, once, clearly. Not a feed of noise; the one thing that changes the next decision.
  • Stay correctable. Show the reasoning, hold uncertainty honestly, and let the person override or hand it back. Reactive is not autonomous.

Why it works

A trader shouldn't have to ask whether a setup is forming — the system sees the pattern and says so, at the moment it forms. A construction team shouldn't have to query a schedule to learn it slipped — the risk surfaces itself. The value moves from "answers when asked" to "notices when it counts."

I design these systems the same way each time: figure out the handful of moments that actually change a decision, then make the product react at exactly those moments — and stay quiet the rest of the time.

The short version

Don't wait for the prompt. Watch the work, act at the moment, stay correctable.
